Tech Empower

Tech Empower Trello board (https://trello.com/b/NqVLW18P/tech-empower3)

**What the charter covers** Team purpose and vision (bridging the digital
divide in under-resourced Cape Town high schools), roles, ways of working
(Kanban on Trello, daily stand-ups, 2-week planning, review and retrospective),
working agreements, communication rules, decision-making, Definition of Done,
core values, risks, and a review process.

**My Role** I was the Developer / Writer. My responsibilities were content creation,
documentation and technical writing. For the charter, I helped write up the team's agreements
in clear language so that all five members could follow them, and I contributed
to discussions on how we would work together.

**What I learned:** - A charter turns vague expectations into agreements everyone can point to.
  For example, our Definition of Done (card in the "Done" column, documentation
  attached, Product Owner's "Final Approval" comment) removed arguments about
  when work was actually finished.
- Our consensus-based decision-making, with the Team Leader as tie-breaker,
  showed me how clear rules keep a team moving when opinions differ.
- Writing the communication norms (WhatsApp for urgent issues, Trello for
  tracking, 4-hour response time) made me aware of how remote work can break
  down without them.
- As the team's writer, I learned that documentation only helps if it is clear
  and short enough that people actually use it. Rules such as the Definition of
  Done are easy to apply because they are written as simple checks.

**Challenge and how I handled it:** One challenge we identified in the charter was working with limited mobile data
and inconsistent school schedules. We handled this by agreeing that Trello
tracks all tasks, that urgent issues go through WhatsApp, and that team members
respond within 4 hours during school hours, so work could continue even when
we could not meet.

**How it connects to later work:** The charter shaped everything after it. The
Trello workflow, user stories, backlogs and logbook all follow the agreements we
made here.
For my own tasks, the charter meant I knew where to record work (Trello cards),
what "finished" looked like (documentation attached and approved by the Product
Owner), and who to go to for decisions.

**What I would improve:** Next time I would review the charter earlier and more often, rather than only
at retrospectives, so that the working agreements are adjusted as soon as they
stop fitting how the team actually works.
